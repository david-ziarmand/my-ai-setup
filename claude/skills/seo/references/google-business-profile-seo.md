# Google Business Profile SEO

Use this reference when the audit or request is about a local business: Google Business Profile, the map pack,
citations, reviews, or location pages.

Help local customers find the right business and turn searches into qualified calls, bookings, and visits. Diagnose the
specific location and search context before prescribing changes.

## Start With the Business Model

Read the project `AGENTS.md`, `docs/`, and the brief first. Ask only for missing facts relevant to the request:

- Business type: customers visit a storefront, staff travel to customers, both, or online only.
- Actual operating locations, staffed hours, service coverage, services, and capacity.
- Website and profile URLs; ownership, verification, duplicates, suspensions, or recent moves.
- Desired outcome and available evidence: qualified calls, bookings, visits, profile performance, Search Console, and
  location-level conversion data.

Use the requested scope. A profile description request needs a description, not a compulsory full audit. Without access,
provide a provisional plan and identify the evidence needed; never report invented profile findings.

For profile eligibility, address visibility, duplicates, categories, and current feature availability,
see [Profiles and Citations](#profiles-and-citations). An online-only business needs a different SEO plan. A service
area is not a collection of extra storefronts.

## Diagnose the Local Search Problem

Keep the two surfaces separate:

| Surface                 | Inspect                                                             | Evidence to retain                                                    |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Maps / local pack       | Correct profile, query relevance, searcher distance, reputation     | Query, observation date, search location, device/context, profile URL |
| Organic website results | Indexation, relevant location/service page, content, internal links | Query, page URL, Search Console data where available                  |

Google describes local ranking through relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance varies with the searcher's
location; a larger declared service area does not establish a nearer physical location. Do not promise a top-three
placement or a fixed ranking uplift.

Compare like with like: the same queries, locations, observation method, and time window. A single search from the
owner's office is not evidence of visibility throughout a city. Separate branded from non-branded demand and label
estimates, observed facts, and hypotheses.

Prioritize the constraint supported by evidence:

1. Wrong, ineligible, duplicate, unverified, or suspended profile: resolve the actual issue before adding promotional
   content.
2. Customers cannot contact or visit correctly: repair location facts, hours, destination links, and conversion paths.
3. Relevant demand is not represented: improve accurate categories, service information, and useful location pages.
4. Weak trust or discovery: address genuine customer feedback, relevant citations, and earned local mentions.

## Improve the Profile and Citation Record

Use [Profiles and Citations](#profiles-and-citations) for the requested profile or listing work. Produce field-level
edits grounded in real business facts, with the existing value, proposed value, reason, and missing verification.

Build a canonical location record for the real-world name, public contact details, customer-facing hours, profile ID,
and matching location URL. Keep a private operating address separate when customers do not visit it. Never publish a
hidden home address merely to satisfy a citation checklist.

Citation cleanup should resolve conflicting identities and customer-facing information. Prioritize relevant maps,
local/chamber, and industry listings customers actually use. Correct existing records and merge true duplicates through
supported processes before creating more listings.

## Earn and Respond to Reviews

Ask customers with genuine experiences for honest feedback through a neutral request and the correct location's review
link. Do not select only happy customers, buy reviews, offer incentives, require particular wording, or promise removal
of criticism. Read the
current [Maps review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB).

Example request: "Thanks for choosing [business]. If you'd like to share your experience, you can leave a review
here: [location review link]. We welcome your honest feedback."

Reply specifically and courteously. For a complaint, acknowledge the concern and offer an appropriate private contact
path without exposing customer, medical, financial, or job details. Report a policy violation through the platform's
process; an unfavorable genuine opinion is not automatically removable.

Track recurring feedback and service improvements alongside review volume and recency. A rating target must not become a
staff quota for soliciting specified review content.

## Build Useful Location and Service Pages

See [Location Pages and Measurement](#location-pages-and-measurement) when building pages, preparing local schema, or
measuring results.

Give each real location a useful destination. A service-area page describes verified service coverage; it must not imply
an office that does not exist. Publish a city page only when it adds meaningful local information, evidence, or customer
utility. Swapping city names in duplicated text does not create that value.

## Return a Decision and an Actionable Artifact

For an audit or plan, return:

1. Business model and the specific location/search surface assessed.
2. Findings with source, date, customer impact, confidence, and missing evidence.
3. Prioritized changes with a responsible owner and a way to verify completion.
4. The requested artifact: field edits, citation cleanup table, review drafts, location-page outline, or measurement
   plan.
5. Follow-up measures tied to qualified customer actions rather than rankings alone.

For narrow requests, deliver the artifact directly and state only the relevant assumptions. Drafting a plan does not
itself authorize profile changes, messages to customers, or directory submissions; follow the user's existing
authorization for execution.

## Profiles and Citations

Use this for profile eligibility, field changes, and name/address/phone cleanup. Verify facts with the owner and the
current [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

### Model the Real Business

| Business                                 | Profile approach                                                    | Common mistake                                                     |
|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Staffed storefront receiving customers   | Actual location, public address and customer-facing hours           | Keyword-stuffed name or wrong entrance                             |
| Service-area business visiting customers | Actual base with address hidden when customers are not served there | Profiles for every city or exposing a home address                 |
| Hybrid                                   | Real customer-facing location plus actual coverage                  | Claiming unstaffed locations                                       |
| Multiple locations                       | Verify each location and its staffing/eligibility separately        | Assuming a location page or rented mailbox establishes eligibility |
| Online only                              | Use organic/AI search strategy appropriate to the business          | Creating a fictitious local presence                               |

Exceptions for practitioners, departments, co-located businesses, and special business types require their specific
rules. Do not merge two legitimately distinct entities just because they share an address. An actual relocation needs an
update to the existing business identity rather than an unexplained new duplicate.

### Field-Level Review

- **Name:** match real-world branding. Put services and coverage in their proper fields rather than adding search
  keywords to the name.
- **Categories:** use the most specific accurate primary category and only justified additional categories. Check the
  current category options for that market.
- **Services:** describe services actually offered; distinguish availability, territory, and appointment requirements.
- **Description:** write factual business/service information. Keep promotions and links out of this field.
- **Hours and contact:** confirm regular/special hours, correct phone routing, and a location-specific working
  website/booking link.
- **Photos:** propose real exterior/entrance, interior, team, or service imagery appropriate to the business. Confirm
  permission before publishing identifiable customers.
- **Posts:** use them for relevant current updates, offers, or events where available; do not invent a posting-frequency
  ranking benefit.
- **Q&A:**
  Google [discontinued its Q&A API on November 3, 2025](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/qanda/change-log).
  Do not prescribe API seeding or a universal editable Q&A section. Verify current interface availability; answer common
  customer questions in website content and accurate profile fields when a profile feature is unavailable.

For service areas, confirm actual coverage
using [Google's service-area guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481). Listing another town does
not create a new location or remove searcher distance as a ranking factor.

### Citation Cleanup Table

Use one row per observed listing:

| Location/entity | Listing URL | Observed public facts | Verified source of truth | Correction / duplicate action | Access/status | Verification date |
|-----------------|-------------|-----------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------|-------------------|

Compare meaning, not merely punctuation: an abbreviation need not be an identity conflict. A wrong phone, old address,
mismatched business, or duplicate that diverts customers deserves priority. Preserve location distinctions and
legitimate branch phone numbers.

For a home-based service business, use supported hidden-address listings or skip platforms that require publishing
private information. Maintain the private operating address separately from public citation data.

Select listings for customer relevance and accurate discovery. Bulk low-quality links, lifetime domain metrics, or a
directory count alone do not demonstrate improved local outcomes. DIY corrections are a valid path; paid listing
management is optional when its coverage, control, cost, and verification process fit the business.

## Location Pages and Measurement

Use this when the request includes local pages, schema, or performance reporting.

### Page Content That Helps a Local Customer

For a real customer-facing location, include accurate location/contact details, hours, services available there,
booking/call links, access/parking directions where known, staff or location evidence, and useful nearby/service
information. Link it from a location index and relevant service pages.

For service coverage without an office, explain actual availability, constraints, travel or call-out policies, relevant
local work examples when available, and a clear enquiry path. Say that it is a service area. Do not show a fake street
address or imply a staffed branch.

Example decision: a plumber has one operating base and serves two nearby towns. One honest coverage page may suffice.
Separate town pages are justified only by additional useful information or evidence; neither town automatically merits
another Business Profile.

Before scaling, confirm a stable data source and meaningful differences across pages. Check the
current [doorway-abuse policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-abuse).
Consolidate pages when the proposed variants add no value.

### Local Structured Data

Use the most specific accurate LocalBusiness subtype and a stable identifier for each real location. Match the visible
page facts; connect the location URL, public phone, hours, and physical coordinates where applicable. Do not mark
service cities as invented branches or publish a hidden residential address through JSON-LD.

Important distinction: Schema.org can describe a business without satisfying Google's rich-result requirements.
Google's [LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)
requires an address for that search feature. For a business that cannot publish one, preserve privacy and explain that
the rich-result requirement is unmet; do not fabricate an address or promise eligibility from `areaServed` alone.

Self-serving LocalBusiness review markup does not earn review stars merely because the business displays testimonials.
Do not import a profile rating into markup as a shortcut to stars.

Use [schema-markup.md](schema-markup.md) for implementation. Validate the rendered page and relevant test tools, then
verify deployed markup matches the public content. A stripped text fetch is insufficient proof that JavaScript-injected
JSON-LD is absent. Validation does not guarantee display or a local-pack ranking change.

### Measure Customer Outcomes

Keep a baseline by location and period:

| Metric                             | Evidence                                      | Interpretation limits                            |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Qualified calls/bookings/leads     | CRM or owner-confirmed outcomes               | Deduplicate; attribution may be incomplete       |
| Profile interactions               | Available Business Profile performance export | Interaction is not a completed sale              |
| Organic local queries/pages        | Search Console query/page reports             | Privacy thresholds and aggregation limit detail  |
| Website actions from profile links | Analytics with consistent campaign tags       | Consent, blockers, and tagging can affect counts |
| Local visibility                   | Comparable query/location observations        | Distance, personalization, and volatility matter |

Use a consistent tagging convention for profile website links while keeping the canonical location URL stable. Test that
redirects and booking flows preserve the attribution needed. For call tracking, retain a genuine reachable business
contact and verify the platform's current phone rules; do not blindly replace canonical citation numbers.

Review changes against a comparable period, seasonality, opening-hours changes, and service capacity. Record what
changed and when. Treat a visibility increase as an observation, not proof of causation or additional revenue. Set
follow-up timing with the user rather than promising a universal recovery deadline.
